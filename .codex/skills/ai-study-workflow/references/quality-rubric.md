# Quality Rubric

Use this rubric to judge whether a study workflow is likely to improve outcomes.

## Strong Workflow

- Starts with a source-quality pass when files are provided.
- Starts review from a diagnostic, and starts first-time learning from a small teachable chunk plus a light readiness check.
- Makes the student answer before showing model answers.
- Uses active recall, practice testing, and spaced review.
- Converts mistakes into source-tagged cards.
- Includes timed practice for exam preparation.
- Escalates uncertain grading, exam scope, or policy questions to a human source.
- Marks sparse extraction, conflicting examples, or missing formulas as `needs human check`.
- Uses interaction pauses for diagnostics, near-transfer, transfer, and mock tests.

## Weak Workflow

- Treats OCR/PDF extraction as complete without checking quality.
- Mostly summarizes notes.
- Lets the student passively read AI output.
- Shows questions and answers together when the user is trying to practice.
- Creates long cards with multiple ideas.
- Has no score, time, mistake, or retention signal.
- Assumes AI knows the professor's exam priorities.
- Uses restricted materials or violates course AI rules.
- Ignores source conflicts or unclear exam scope.

## Measurement

Track at least one outcome per session:

- diagnostic score
- mock score
- time per question
- number of recurring mistake types
- number of hard cards remaining
- ability to explain without notes
- number of `needs human check` items resolved
